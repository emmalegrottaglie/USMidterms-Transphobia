# Context: anti-trans political advertising in the 2026 US midterms

Background for the midterms study (general election 3 November 2026), shown on the site's Context tab.

These figures come from other organisations, not from this project's data. This project counts Twitter posts; it does not measure advertising. The totals below come from different sources with different scopes and dates, so they are not added together.

**How this was checked.** The items come from the Bot Traffic Monitor's `docs/MIDTERM-ADS.md`, written by a research agent on 16 November 2026. Each item below was checked by hand on its source page on 18 November 2026, and the corrections found then are applied here. Items in that document that were not checked by hand (the PolitiFact rulings, Semafor, KSAT, the OECD incident record, AP-NORC, the AP Voter Poll and Ground Media) are left out.

## How much was spent

- **AdImpact, reported by AP (28 October 2026).** Republicans aired transgender-themed ads about 217,000 times, for an estimated $183M up to 25 October 2026. At the same point in the 2024 general election the figure was about $151M. Democrats spent about $11M on transgender-related ads. Source: [AP story by Peoples and Kessler, republished by News4JAX](https://www.news4jax.com/news/politics/2026/10/28/republicans-emphasize-transgender-ads-putting-cultural-issues-over-voters-economic-anxiety/).
- **Wesleyan Media Project (13 August 2026).** The release names "attacks on transgender people" as the tenth most common topic in pro-Republican House ads, in 14% of airings, from 1 January 2025 to 2 August 2026. Its Table 2 labels the matching row "LGBTQ Issues / Rights" (13.9%), listed about twelfth (re-checked on the page on 18 November 2026). Source: [Wesleyan Media Project release](https://mediaproject.wesleyan.edu/releases-081326/).
- **Metro Weekly (11 November 2026).** About $187M across nine states (Arizona, Colorado, Georgia, Missouri, Nebraska, Ohio, Texas, Washington and Wisconsin). The article names no data source for this figure and does not attribute it to GLAAD. Source: [Metro Weekly](https://www.metroweekly.com/2026/11/anti-trans-ads-midterm-voters/).
- **Human Rights Campaign estimate, reported by Metro Weekly (11 November 2026).** The Human Rights Campaign estimated that "nearly $400 million was spent nationwide on anti-transgender ads this election cycle". This is a separate estimate with a different scope from the nine-state figure above; the two are not compared or added. Source: [Metro Weekly](https://www.metroweekly.com/2026/11/anti-trans-ads-midterm-voters/).

## Who paid, and where

- **Texas Senate race.** Transgender-themed ads aired an estimated 50,000 times, at a cost of $58M, up to 25 October 2026 (AdImpact, reported by AP). AP describes Texas PAC as "largely funded by Elon Musk". Source: [AP via News4JAX](https://www.news4jax.com/news/politics/2026/10/28/republicans-emphasize-transgender-ads-putting-cultural-issues-over-voters-economic-anxiety/).
- **Musk, the Senate Leadership Fund and Texas PAC.** Musk gave the Senate Leadership Fund $75M on 9 September and $83M on 23 September 2026. The Senate Leadership Fund gave Texas PAC $75M on 10 September and $83M on 24 September. Source: [CNN, republished by KRDO (16 October 2026)](https://krdo.com/news/2026/10/16/musk-contributed-more-than-150-million-to-bolster-gop-in-texas-senate-race-filings-show/).
- **Ohio Senate race.** AP gives nearly $26M as the cost of all attacks on Sherrod Brown by Republican groups led by the Senate Leadership Fund (not by the Senate Leadership Fund alone). Source: [AP via News4JAX](https://www.news4jax.com/news/politics/2026/10/28/republicans-emphasize-transgender-ads-putting-cultural-issues-over-voters-economic-anxiety/).
- **America PAC.** The Advocate, citing The New York Times (8 September 2026), reported that each of America PAC's initial midterm ads focused on transgender issues; they targeted Talarico, Brown, Turek and Peltola. The initial ads were reported on 8 and 9 September. Source: [The Advocate (9 September 2026)](https://www.advocate.com/politics/election/elon-musk-pac-transgender-attacks).
- **America PAC spending.** More than $3.7M on mostly digital or streaming ads as of 23 September 2026 (AdImpact). FactCheck.org says a number of these ads attacked candidates on transgender issues, not all of them. Source: [FactCheck.org](https://www.factcheck.org/2026/09/america-pac-2/).

## Voters

- **GLAAD and Morning Consult poll, 4 to 6 November 2026.** 2,026 registered voters, of whom 1,725 voted in the midterms. Among those midterm voters, 37% said they were less likely and 18% more likely to support a candidate who ran ads about transgender issues. Source: [Metro Weekly (11 November 2026)](https://www.metroweekly.com/2026/11/anti-trans-ads-midterm-voters/).
- **Human Rights Campaign poll by GQR, reported by Erin Reed (Erin in the Morning, 16 November 2026).** After the election, 65% of voters said they wanted to hear less in campaigns about "trans athletes and surgeries" and 14% more; 58% agreed that Republicans' "obsession" with transgender people and children is "strange and over the top". The article gives no sample size, fieldwork dates or population beyond "voters". Source: [Erin in the Morning](https://www.erininthemorning.com/p/voters-found-republican-trans-obsession).

## Added on 20 November 2026

Emma asked for reporting from trans-positive outlets and trans journalists. These items were read on their source pages on 20 November 2026. ### On Twitter

- **Uncloseted Media and The 19th (Hope Pisoni, 21 October 2026).** Posts attacking James Talarico on Twitter after he won the Texas Democratic Senate primary on 3 March 2026. In the three days before the win the newsrooms found 4 posts about his positions on trans issues; in the 26 hours after it, 182 anti-trans posts attacking him (63 citing the "nonbinary" clip, 50 the sermon clip, 35 the "sexes" clip). Between 10 p.m. ET on 3 March and 11 a.m. ET on 4 March they found 48 posts with similar talking points and no pro-trans posts. Of the posts analysed, 12.1% came from accounts tied to the Republican Party or the Trump administration, 18.7% from right-wing media figures and 18.1% from influencers and commentators. The article does not describe how posts were found, gives no method for its "over 10 million people" reach estimate (left out here), and treats coordination differently by version: the Uncloseted Media version, republished by LGBTQ Nation on 27 October 2026 (pasted by Emma), is subtitled "200 X posts suggest a coordinated effort" and says the spike "shows how an army of accounts works together", while The 19th's version says neither. The body gives no direct evidence of coordination, and the experts it quotes disagree. That version also carries an infographic of which accounts reposted which source account's clips; it is not reproduced here. These posts are from March, before this project's collection began. Sources: [Uncloseted Media](https://unclosetedmedia.com/p/how-republicans-manufactured-anti), [The 19th](https://19thnews.org/2026/10/republicans-anti-trans-backlash-james-talarico/).

- **GUDEA, reported by LGBTQ Nation (Alex Bollinger, 13 October 2026).** The data firm GUDEA analysed 188,631 posts about trans women in the WNBA from 20 July to 12 August 2026; 99% were on Twitter. "Non-typical" accounts (high-visibility influencers, pundits and aggregators) were 3.57% of participants but wrote 16.75% of the posts, which GUDEA reads as influencers pushing the topic for engagement. The article does not say how accounts were classified, and its claims about "inauthentic engagement" are GUDEA's, quoted from Rolling Stone; they are left out. LGBTQ Nation refused automated reads; Emma pasted the article on 20 November 2026. Sources: [LGBTQ Nation](https://www.lgbtqnation.com/2026/10/this-summers-wnba-trans-debate-was-pushed-on-social-media-users-for-money-report-suggests/), [Rolling Stone, cited by it](https://www.rollingstone.com/culture/culture-features/wnba-trans-athlete-debate-gudea-report-1235636879/).

### Fake messages in a trans group's name

- **The Texas Tribune (Ayden Runnels, 14 October 2026).** Texts promoting the Transgender Education Network of Texas's guides for trans students were sent from at least three numbers on the evenings of 12 and 13 October 2026, and appeared aimed at self-identified Republicans. The group said it did not send them and did not know who did, and sent a cease-and-desist letter to two messaging companies. A Republican state representative asked the attorney general to investigate the group. Source: [The Texas Tribune](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/10/14/texas-transgender-texts-cease-desist/).
- **LGBTQ Nation (John Russell, 23 October 2026), citing The Advocate.** About a week after the texts, a "Texas Trans & LGBTQ+ Student Resource Guide" from a "Texas Transgender Student Union" (an LLC formed in Wyoming on 2 September 2026, with no online presence) began arriving in Texas mailboxes, coinciding with the start of early voting on 19 October. Conservatives began posting about it on social media "earlier this week" (the week of 19 October). The guide carried TENT's web address; TENT said it did not send it. On Thursday 22 October TENT's lawyer, Mimi Marziani, said TENT believes the texts and mailers are one campaign by a single entity or payer, appear aimed at Republicans and were timed to early voting; TENT petitioned a Texas court to allow depositions to identify who is behind it. The article links a Texas Tribune story of 20 October that ties the mailers to a mail-tracking company; that story was not read. LGBTQ Nation refused automated reads; Emma pasted the article on 20 November 2026. Source: [LGBTQ Nation](https://www.lgbtqnation.com/2026/10/mystery-mailers-impersonating-trans-org-provoke-conservative-outrage-the-org-is-fighting-back/).

### Harm to trans people

- **University of Melbourne study, reported by LGBTQ Nation (Greg Owen, 9 October 2026).** "Mental health impacts of anti-trans stigma on transgender people in Australia: a mixed-methods study", Frontiers in Public Health. A survey of 807 trans and gender-diverse Australians aged 16 and over: more than 70% had seen or heard anti-trans stigma online several times a week in the previous month, and compared with those exposed less often they were 33% more likely to report suicidal ideation, 38% more likely to report self-harm and 43% more likely to report suicide planning. The article's "37% and 25%" figures for anxiety and depression are worded ambiguously and are left out. The study itself was not read. Emma pasted the article on 20 November 2026. Sources: [LGBTQ Nation](https://www.lgbtqnation.com/2026/10/the-flood-of-online-hate-is-harming-trans-nonbinary-peoples-mental-health-new-study-says/), [the study](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2026.1906134/full).

### Trans writers on the campaign

- **Erin Reed, Erin in the Morning (4 November 2026).** Reed, a trans journalist, tracked 160 races with trans implications and counted 107 won by the trans-supportive side and 53 not, as of the morning after the election. No method is given and some races were not yet called. Her "nearly $200 million" spending figure (sourced to ABC News) is left out because the site already gives AP's $183M and does not compare totals. Source: [Erin in the Morning](https://www.erininthemorning.com/p/a-stunning-rebuke-of-anti-trans-politics).
- **Julia Serano, "Shifting Positions" (5 November 2026).** Serano, a trans writer, documents Democratic candidates, including Senate and governor nominees, who distanced themselves from trans rights in statements and ads, most of them in early September 2026. She notes the statements came within a week or two of one another, which hinted at coordination between campaigns, but says no evidence of it has come to light and that it may have been a snowball effect. This is about Democratic campaigns, not about the anti-trans posts this project counts. Source: [Switch Hitter](https://juliaserano.substack.com/p/shifting-positions-democrats-transgender).

- The Human Rights Campaign's "$280 million" (AdImpact, broadcast and connected TV) and the Transgender Law Center's "over $400 million" (quoted by Prism, no source given): different scopes, and the site does not add or compare totals.
- LGBTQ Nation (Alex Bollinger, 28 October 2026), "Republicans have spent nearly $200 million on anti-trans ads in the midterms" (pasted by Emma on 20 November 2026). Its figures ($183M and 217,000 airings up to 25 October, $151M in 2024, $58M against Talarico, $11M by Democrats) are AdImpact's via AP, already on the site from AP. ### Added from GLAAD's results statement (4 November 2026, pasted by Emma on 20 November 2026)

- **Spending.** GLAAD gives over $187M spent on anti-trans campaign ads, including $78M in Texas and $26M in Ohio, with no named data source or scope. The $187M total matches the Metro Weekly figure above, which also names no source. On the site it is listed under "How much was spent" and not compared with AdImpact's figures. Source: [GLAAD](https://glaad.org/2026-midterm-election-results-anti-trans-fearmongering-fails-as-pro-equality-candidates-win-nationwide/).
- **Trans candidates elected.** Allison Montgomery (Alabama's first out trans state lawmaker), Jaelynn Scott (first out Black trans woman in Washington's state House), Joanna Whaley (Michigan legislature), Bentley Hudgins (Georgia's first out trans and nonbinary lawmaker; GLAAD spells the name "Hudgens", The Advocate and PinkNews "Hudgins"), and Sarah McBride, re-elected to Congress. Source: GLAAD, as above.
- **Left out.** GLAAD's claims that voters elected pro-LGBTQ candidates "in all contested states" that aired anti-trans ads (elsewhere in the same statement, "nearly every"), and its framing of the results as voters rejecting the ads: the statement shows no analysis of cause.

### Three October polls (added 20 November 2026)

Erin Reed, "Trio Of Polls Spell Disaster For GOP Over Anti-Trans Ads", Erin in the Morning, 20 October 2026 (pasted by Emma, who also supplied its [URL](https://www.erininthemorning.com/p/trio-of-polls-spell-disaster-for)). Each poll was checked against its own topline PDF:

- **Navigator Research** (Global Strategy Group and GBAO), 1 to 5 October 2026, 1,375 total interviews with a 1,000 base, registered voters, margin of error ±3.1 points. Asked to pick up to five of 20 issues "most important for the President and Congress to focus on", 4% picked transgender issues, last of the 20; by party 5% Democrats, 3% independents, 4% Republicans. [Topline, page 6](https://navigatorresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Navigator-Toplines-10.05.2026.pdf).
- **AP-NORC**, 8 to 12 October 2026, 1,119 adults, margin of error ±3.9 points. "Thinking about voting in the upcoming election, how important are each of the following issues?": transgender issues 11% one of the most important, 38% important but not one of the most, 51% not an important issue. The highest "not an important issue" share for any other issue listed was 14% (immigration). [Topline](https://apnorc.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/October-2026-Topline-Issues.pdf).
- **ABC News, Washington Post and Ipsos**, 9 to 13 October 2026, 2,545 U.S. adults, Ipsos KnowledgePanel, margin of error ±2 points for the full sample and ±2.8 for half-sample items. Trust to handle transgender issues (a half-sample item): Democrats 38%, Republicans 29%, both equally 5%, neither 27%, skipped 1%. [Topline, page 5](https://www.ipsos.com/sites/default/files/ct/news/documents/2026-10/ABC-Post-Ipsos%20Trend%20Topline%2010.18.pdf).
- LGBTQ Nation (November 2026), "Most Democratic ad buys on trans issues pushed back against Republicans, analysis finds", on an analysis by the Transgender Freedom Alliance. The page refused automated reads, and on 20 November 2026 it returned "page not found" to Emma in a browser, so it may have been removed or moved. Search snippets of it give shares whose scope is unclear (2% of Democratic digital and streaming buys from campaigns taking anti-trans positions, and 7.3% described as potentially anti-trans), and the analysis itself was not found. Left out until the original analysis can be read. The Transgender Freedom Alliance's September 2026 messaging memo (Global Strategy Group, HIT Strategies and Equis Research), which Emma also supplied, is advice for Democratic campaigns, not data on the campaign, and is left out as well.
